Aims of the lesson & Lead-in

Aims of the lesson

After the lesson, you will be able to:

📚Knowledge
  • use the topic words and chunks of 4 groups: clothes, looks and attitudes, shopping and choosing, the fashion industry;
  • put the object in the right place with phrasal verbs (try it on, take them back);
  • compare with comparatives, superlatives and adverbs of degree (far more, by far the most, nowhere near as … as).
🛠️Skills
  • read B2 texts for the main idea, details, reference and inference;
  • listen to announcements, a conversation and a talk for key information;
  • answer VSTEP Speaking Parts 1–3 about fashion and appearance at work;
  • write an informal email (Task 1) and an opinion essay (Task 2).
💡Attitude
  • talk about personal taste with confidence and respect other people's style;
  • think critically about fashion, health and the choices we make as shoppers;
  • use only English during speaking activities.

Grammar & sentence patterns

Grammar 1 · Phrasal verbs — where does the object go?

Three rules
🔀SEPARABLE — OBJECT IS A NOUN

With a separable phrasal verb, a noun object can go after the particle or in between — both are correct.

  • I tried on the red jacket.
  • I tried the red jacket on.
  • She took back the dress. = She took the dress back.
📌OBJECT IS A PRONOUN — ALWAYS IN THE MIDDLE

A pronoun (it, them, him …) must go between the verb and the particle.

  • I tried it on. I tried on it.
  • Take them off. Take off them.
  • She gave it back. She gave back it.
🔗THREE-WORD VERBS — NEVER SEPARATED

A three-word phrasal verb keeps the order verb + particle + particle + object.

  • get away with casual stuff
  • keep up with the latest fashions
  • fit in with my colleagues
Corpus spot
  • The verb in a phrasal verb is still irregular in the past tense: took off (not taked off), went out, stood out, put together.

Grammar 2 · Comparing — adjectives, adverbs and degree

Six rules
📏-ER / MORE
  • One-syllable adjectives add -er / -est.
  • Adjectives of two or more syllables use more / the most.
  • Spelling — ending in -e: just add -r / -st.
  • Spelling — vowel + consonant: double the consonant.
  • Spelling — consonant + y: change to -ier / -iest.
  • old → older → the oldest
  • nice → nicer → the nicest
  • big → bigger · slim → slimmer
  • dirty → dirtier · skinny → the skinniest
  • casual → more casual → the most casual
🔁BOTH FORMS ARE CORRECT
  • Some two-syllable adjectives can take either -er/-est or more/most.
  • simple → simpler / more simple
  • common · likely · narrow
  • pleasant · polite · gentle
⚠️IRREGULAR
  • Don't add -er.
  • Don't add more.
  • good → better → the best
  • bad → worse → the worst
  • far → farther / further → the farthest / furthest
  • old → older / elder → the oldest / eldest
📉LESS / THE LEAST · NOT AS … AS
  • less … than and the least … are the opposite of more / the most.
  • not as … as = not as … as (not so … as is rarer).
  • less serious than · less expensive than
  • the least achievable figure
  • Sally is not as tall as her brother.
  • These shoes don't look as comfortable as the flip flops.
🎚️ADVERBS OF DEGREE
  • Before a comparative: a bit · slightly · a little · a lot · much · far · rather · a great deal.
  • Before a superlative: by far · easily · much · quite.
  • He is much older than me.
  • your feet are a lot bigger than mine
  • slightly better value for money
  • by far the most creative student
  • easily the biggest animal in the world
🏃COMPARING ADVERBS
  • Adverbs ending in -ly: more / less + adverb.
  • Short adverbs add -er.
  • Some adverbs are irregular.
  • more commonly · more readily available
  • less seriously · less exclusively
  • well → better · badly → worse
  • fast → faster · hard → harder
Used with
  • than after a comparative: She is two years older than me.
  • the before a superlative: It was the happiest day of my life.
  • Common mistakes: the best clothes (not the better clothes) · a much better one (not much more better) · easier (not more easy).

Model

If I were you, I'd go for a plain shirt and dark trousers rather than jeans — it looks far more professional. Model answer

I'd say yes, but they can't do it alone. There's no getting away from the fact that counterfeit goods are illegal, and fake cosmetics can even be dangerous. They also have a negative impact on the genuine brands, which lose sales. Admittedly, many people buy knock-offs because designer labels are far too expensive. However, the more people buy fakes, the more the trade grows. So the best option would be to fine sellers and, at the same time, raise awareness of who really pays for a cheap copy. Shoppers should weigh up the options — a well-made item from a smaller brand is often better value for money.
